# Turn a weakness into Commons value

Critical thinking strengthens Cora Commons when a verified gap becomes a safe, testable improvement. Agents should help participants move from **observe -> verify -> propose -> agree -> build -> review -> reuse**.

## The constructive improvement path

1. **Observe:** state the weakness precisely and identify who or what it could harm.
2. **Verify:** cite the current canonical source. Separate fact, inference and unknown.
3. **Search:** check existing Needs, issues, pull requests and BRICKS to avoid duplicate work.
4. **Frame the outcome:** describe what should become true without assuming a particular implementation.
5. **Design the smallest safe fix:** name one bounded artifact, what is out of scope, dependencies, rights or conflicts, known cost and important unknowns.
6. **Define proof:** state observable acceptance evidence and who could review it independently.
7. **Ask the human:** wait for explicit approval before posting, building, publishing, spending or using private information.
8. **Contribute:** open an [improvement proposal](https://github.com/jamessunheart/cora-commons/issues/new?template=improvement.yml), then work in a fork or focused branch after agreement.
9. **Preserve learning:** after independent acceptance, create a Contribution Receipt and suggest the next reuse.

## What good agent behavior sounds like

> I found a possible gap in the contributor-rights boundary. The repository is MIT-licensed, but the treatment of private knowledge and non-code resource offers is not explicit. I found no existing accepted BRICK that resolves it. The smallest useful improvement is a plain-language contributor-rights policy with tests consisting of four realistic contribution scenarios. Estimated effort is two human review hours plus drafting. This is a proposal, not legal advice or an accepted policy. Would you like me to prepare the public issue draft?

## Guardrails

- Do not suppress criticism merely to sound constructive.
- Do not invent a solution when evidence is incomplete; a well-scoped Need is valuable.
- Do not present a draft as policy, a test as real-world usefulness, or attribution as reward.
- Do not expose private information to prove a weakness.
- Do not let the contributor approve their own work.
- Do not post or begin substantial work until the directing human agrees.
