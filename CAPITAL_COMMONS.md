# Capital Commons build path

The Capital Commons is the part of Cora Commons that helps verified resources move toward agreed needs without pretending that every promise, contribution or CORA record is spendable capital.

## What counts as capital

Capital may include:

- accepted intelligence and reusable BRICKS;
- human time or professional capacity committed under an agreement;
- compute, tools, equipment, space or other usable resources;
- distribution, trusted introductions and verified demand;
- donated or sponsored fiat held through an authorized legal and accounting path;
- funded products or services explicitly available to CORA holders.

Unverified promises, activity counts, speculative token prices, inaccessible assets and unfunded CORA do **not** count as available capital.

## Three records that must remain distinct

1. **Agreement record** — what outcome was requested, who agreed to do what, evidence required, limits, costs and expected return.
2. **Contribution receipt** — what was actually delivered, who and which agents contributed, independent acceptance, reuse, impact and current reward status.
3. **Resource and allocation record** — what usable resource is actually available, its owner/steward, restrictions, reservation, delivery and reconciliation.

Keeping these separate prevents an offer from being mistaken for delivery, attribution from becoming an unfunded debt, and a provisional CORA calculation from being presented as spendable value.

## Required state transitions

Contribution:

`PROPOSED -> AGREED -> DELIVERED -> ACCEPTED -> REUSED -> IMPACT VERIFIED -> REWARD ALLOCATED`

Resource:

`DECLARED -> VERIFIED -> COMMITTED -> RESERVED -> DELIVERED -> RECONCILED`

CORA:

`UNASSESSED -> PROVISIONAL CALCULATION -> FUNDED OR SERVICE-BACKED -> AVAILABLE -> SPENT OR SETTLED`

No automated process may skip an independent review or the required human authorization for funding, allocation, payment or publication.

## Build order

### Phase 1 — Evidence foundation

Build the append-only Contribution Receipt schema and deterministic validator. Every later calculation depends on trustworthy attribution, evidence and cost records.

### Phase 2 — Coordination foundation

Build agreement and work-claim records plus an independent review queue. This lets many human-agent teams coordinate without duplicating work or approving themselves.

### Phase 3 — Capital inventory

Build a public-safe catalog of real resources and utility offers. Track availability and restrictions without exposing credentials, private beneficiary data or treasury access.

### Phase 4 — Economic simulation

Build a versioned CORA calculation simulator using accepted receipts, reuse and impact evidence. Outputs remain provisional and non-transferable until separately funded or service-backed.

### Phase 5 — Bounded utility pilot

Run one small pilot in which a stated pool of compute, services or Zen Village capacity is available under explicit limits. Test contribution, earning, access, settlement and replenishment as separate events.

### Phase 6 — Governed allocation and scale

Only after the pilot works, add authenticated identities, scoped write actions, allocation proposals, approval thresholds, audit logs, disputes, federation between local Commons and capacity/load testing.

## Current priority

Build Needs 0002, 0003 and 0004 first. They create the evidence and coordination layer required before resource accounting or CORA simulation can be trusted.

The first proof is not a large balance or token price. It is a stranger and their agent completing useful work, receiving durable attribution, passing independent review and helping another participant start further ahead.
