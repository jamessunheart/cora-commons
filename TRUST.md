# Contributor rights, independent review and disputes

This founding policy makes the current boundary explicit. It is a working Commons policy, not legal or tax advice, and should receive qualified review before high-value intellectual property, regulated activity or material capital enters the Commons.

## Rights to repository contributions

- Contributors retain ownership of original work they create.
- By intentionally submitting code or documentation for inclusion in this repository, a contributor confirms they are authorized to share it and licenses the submitted material under the repository's [MIT License](LICENSE).
- A submission must identify important third-party material and its license. Restricted, copied or confidential material must not be submitted.
- A repository submission does not transfer trademarks, personal identity rights, private datasets, physical property, hosting accounts, money, services or background intellectual property that is not contained in the submission.
- Resource offers, private knowledge, commercial services and physical assets require a separate explicit agreement describing permitted use, duration, revocation, privacy and expected return.
- When rights are uncertain, stop at a private-safe proposal and obtain the rights holder's and, when appropriate, qualified counsel's review.

## Who counts as independent

An independent reviewer:

- did not create, operate or materially direct the contribution;
- is not receiving the proposed reward from the same contribution;
- discloses financial, employment, family, reciprocal-review or other relationships that could affect judgment;
- tests against the agreement's acceptance evidence and records limitations;
- does not treat automated checks alone as proof of `USABLE`.

Reciprocal approval between two contributors is not sufficient by itself when the reviews affect recognition, allocation or governance. A disclosed reciprocal relationship requires a second unconflicted reviewer or maintainer decision. The public reviewer pool and conflict checks remain open work in Need 0004.

## Disputes and appeals

Until a dedicated dispute service exists:

1. Keep disputed work in `NEEDS_REVISION` or `REJECTED`; it must not become an accepted BRICK or funded reward.
2. Open a public-safe issue labeled `dispute` linking the agreement, delivery and review evidence. Remove private or legally sensitive details.
3. State the contested decision, evidence, requested remedy and conflicts of interest.
4. A moderator who was neither contributor nor original reviewer examines the record. Material conflicts require recusal.
5. Record the outcome and reasoning without deleting the earlier history. Possible outcomes are `UPHELD`, `REVIEW_REOPENED`, `REASSIGNED` or `INSUFFICIENT_PUBLIC_EVIDENCE`.
6. Treasury, legal, privacy or safety disputes remain paused until an authorized person or qualified professional resolves the relevant boundary.

This process is deliberately conservative. It creates an auditable appeal path but does not claim to be a court, arbitration agreement or substitute for applicable law.

## Economic honesty

Attribution and an accepted receipt do not create money, debt, equity, ownership, tax treatment or guaranteed future CORA. Every funded or service-backed pilot must publish the available pool, custodian, eligibility, pricing or allocation rule, depletion condition and settlement evidence before participants rely on it.
